The Auto-Pédestre Dalheim is a 7.9 km loop walk around Dalheim in the Guttland, the centre of the country. It has 100 m of climbing in total and stays between 289 and 377 m above sea level. At a steady walking pace it takes about 2 hours. Apart from Dalheim, there is no other village on the way.

The route in detail

About 34% of the distance is on tarmac, 41% on farm and forest tracks and 25% on narrow paths. 57% of the route runs through woodland. In the direction of the GPX track, the longest climb gains 88 m between km 1.5 and km 6.1; the steepest 500 m, from km 2.9, average 7%. The highest point, 377 m, comes at km 6.1.

Along the way

OpenStreetMap lists 6 benches, a picnic spot and a shelter within 60 m of the route. Places to eat right by the route: Café du Centre (km 8.6).

When to go

With 57% woodland, much of the route is in shade, which helps on hot days. 66% of the route is unpaved and can be muddy after rain and in winter; sturdy shoes help.

Compared with the trails nearby

Compared with the 5 nearest circuits, this one has the least climbing and has the least tarmac. The closest, Auto-Pédestre Moutfort (3.9 km away), is 9.7 km long with 140 m of climbing. For something shorter nearby: Auto-Pédestre Bech-Kleinmacher, 5.5 km. For a longer walk: Auto-Pédestre Mondorf, 9.9 km.
