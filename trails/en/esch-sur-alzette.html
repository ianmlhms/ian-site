This loop around Luxembourg's second city reveals the surprisingly green side of the Minett. From the edge of town the path climbs into the wooded hills around the Ellergronn nature reserve, where forest, ponds and rare species now occupy former mining land.

Iron ore was mined here for over a century; the red Minett earth gave the whole region its name. At Ellergronn, the preserved buildings of the former Cockerill mine recall that era; today the site is a centre for nature education.

The trail climbs through hillside forest in several stretches and rewards you with views over the city, which was European Capital of Culture in 2022. Start and finish connect well to Esch's dense bus and rail network.

The Auto-Pédestre Esch-sur-Alzette is a 7.2 km loop walk around Esch-sur-Alzette in the Minett, the south of the country. It has 150 m of climbing in total and stays between 328 and 417 m above sea level. At a steady walking pace it takes about 2 hours. Apart from Esch-sur-Alzette, there is no other village on the way.

The route in detail

About 35% of the distance is on tarmac, 55% on farm and forest tracks and 10% on narrow paths. 72% of the route runs through woodland. In the direction of the GPX track, the longest climb gains 71 m between km 0.8 and km 3.2; the steepest 500 m, from km 4.2, average 7%. The highest point, 417 m, comes at km 5.5.

Along the way

Within a few hundred metres: Grouwen a Feldbunn (museum, 59 m from the route) and Ellergronn (nature reserve, 310 m from the route). OpenStreetMap lists 37 benches, 13 picnic spots, 5 shelters and 2 playgrounds within 60 m of the route. Places to eat right by the route: Fevi Bosque (restaurant, km 2.8), Camping's Stuff (café, km 3.1), Centre d'accueil Ellergronn (restaurant, km 7) and An der Schmedd (restaurant, km 7).

When to go

With 72% woodland, much of the route is in shade, which helps on hot days. 65% of the route is unpaved and can be muddy after rain and in winter; sturdy shoes help.

Compared with the trails nearby

Among the 5 nearest circuits it is neither the longest nor the shortest, and its 150 m of climbing are not an extreme either. The closest, Auto-Pédestre Rumelange 1 (2.2 km away), is 8.9 km long with 130 m of climbing. For a longer walk: Auto-Pédestre Belvaux, 9.4 km.
